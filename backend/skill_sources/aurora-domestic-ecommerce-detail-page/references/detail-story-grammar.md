# Detail story grammar

## Purchase-decision map

Classify the current product by dominant and supporting logic, not category name alone:

- effect or parameter;
- material or sensory experience;
- structure or durability;
- identity or trust;
- price or specification;
- scene or emotion.
- interaction or control;
- installation or compatibility;
- selection or configuration;
- suite or multi-entity relationship.

List the buyer's most consequential doubts. Each selected module must answer one doubt, advance one conclusion, or close one transaction risk.

## Candidate module duties

These are selectable duties, not a fixed order:

- first-screen value promise;
- core difference;
- mechanism or structure proof;
- material, ingredient or detail proof;
- operation or use process;
- result or experience;
- audience and scenario fit;
- parameter-to-benefit explanation;
- fact-supported comparison or selection reason;
- trust and risk removal;
- SKU, package, compatibility, service or offer closure;
- final value reinforcement.
- directed pathway or process explanation;
- trigger–feedback–result interaction;
- installation, fit or maintenance-access proof;
- buyer-condition-to-option selection;
- suite topology and role explanation.

## Planning rules

- Resolve material contradictions before building claim coverage, screen-count candidates, style, or storyboard. Ask once with all conflicts and wait for the user's retained values; planning against competing values is invalid.
- Set module count from complexity and evidence, normally 6–12 for an ordinary page but allow fewer or more when justified. The range is a diagnostic reference, never a default.
- Build a complete claim-coverage map before choosing the count. Every verified fact must be marked lead, support, context-only, or deferred with a reason.
- Compare at least two plausible grouping options. Choose the smallest count that preserves all purchase-relevant distinctions, evidence, legibility and rhythm; add a module when merging would blur different buyer questions or overload one screen.
- Record the exact merge or separation decisions that produced the final count. A bare statement such as “8 screens is enough” is invalid.
- Use one lead conclusion per module. Combine related supporting points when they form one coherent picture.
- Never repeat the same claim with the same proof and buyer conclusion in adjacent modules.
- Follow a dense rational module with a lower-density scene, sensory, or result module when possible.
- Translate parameters into purchase meaning.
- Keep lower modules commercially finished; do not let them decay into document attachments.
- End with specification, service, offer, trust, or a reinforced buying reason.
- Preserve relevant supplied formula, ingredient, process, use and specification content by grouping it into coherent modules; do not confuse concise selection with broad deletion.
- Treat “visual first” as a division of labor: the image demonstrates the object, action, relation, change, scale, texture or evidence; concise copy names the takeaway, disambiguates the relation, or states an approved figure/condition. Normal sales modules must not be reduced to either a poster-sized headline or a silent decorative picture.
- For every module schedule a composition family, camera scale, text mode, reading path, brand anchor and declared variation from the previous module. Set the independent-logo policy to `REQUIRED_IF_AUTHORIZED` for M01 and `FORBIDDEN` for M02 onward. Later brand anchors must be expressed through visual DNA and product identity, never a repeated standalone logo.
- A centered display headline is valid when it serves the module duty. It is not the default structure for every module.
- Route multi-device, accessory, variant, apparel, and body-contact products through `category-routing-v2.md` before fixing module duties.
- Route pathway, interaction, installation, and selection duties through `advanced-page-archetypes.md`.
- Apply `high-risk-proof-conditions.md` before selecting any quantified result, comparison, time sequence, body claim, expert, certificate, or test proof.

## Transition contract

For every module record:

1. What the previous module established.
2. What question the current module answers.
3. What new question the buyer will naturally have afterward.
4. How the next module receives that question.
